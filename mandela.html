Historical Description & Context

On 20 April 1964, during the Rivonia Trial at the Palace of Justice in Pretoria, Nelson Mandela delivered his famous three-hour statement from the dock as the first accused in State v. Nelson Mandela and Others (Mandela, 1964:1; Frankel, 2011:88). Speaking on behalf of his co-accused ANC and Umkhonto we Sizwe (MK) leadership, Mandela articulated the socio-political grievances of South Africa's disenfranchised majority, explaining the transition from non-violent resistance to armed struggle (Mandela, 1964:4-12; Lodge, 2011:145).

The speech concluded with his iconic declaration of commitment to a democratic and free society, stating that it was an ideal for which he was prepared to die (Mandela, 1964:30; Sampson, 1999:192). The trial proceedings were recorded using Dictabelt technology; these original analog belts were preserved by the National Archives and Records Service of South Africa (NARS) and meticulously digitised and restored in partnership with the French National Audiovisual Institute (INA) (NARS & INA, 2018; UNESCO, 2014).
